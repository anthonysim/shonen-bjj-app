## "Am I too old to start?"

It's the question we hear more than any other from adults, and the honest answer is no. Brazilian Jiu Jitsu is built on leverage, timing and technique rather than size or strength. That's the whole point of the art: it was developed so a smaller, less athletic person could control someone bigger, stronger and faster. Youth and raw athleticism help a little. Good technique helps a lot more, and technique is something you can start learning at any age.

## You don't need to get in shape first

"I'll come in once I'm in better shape" is the most common reason people put off their first class. It's also backwards. Most of our students walked in with zero grappling experience and no particular fitness base, and their conditioning built naturally as they trained.

Adults start in [Gi Basics](/programs/bjj), a 60 minute fundamentals class that runs Monday through Thursday at 7:00 PM. It covers the essential positions and techniques, self-defense, and the balance and flexibility that make everything else easier. You'll be sore after your first few classes. That's normal, and it fades quickly once your body knows what to expect.

## What a class actually looks like

Not knowing what happens once you step on the mat is half the nerves. Every adult class at Shonen follows the same clear structure:

- **Warm-up.** Movement drills that get your body ready to grapple.
- **Technique.** The position of the day, broken down in detail.
- **Drilling.** Reps with a partner, with feedback from the instructor as you go.
- **Positional sparring.** Start from the day's position and work it against resistance.
- **Live rounds.** Timed rounds against a resisting partner, at the level you're ready for.
- **Q&A.** Ask questions and get personal feedback on your game.

Notice the order. By the time anyone is resisting you, you've already seen the move, drilled it and tried it from a set position. Nobody throws you into the deep end on day one.

## How to train smart when you start later

Starting at 35, 45 or 55 doesn't change what you learn. It does change how you should approach it. A few habits make a big difference:

- **Tap early, tap often.** Tapping isn't losing. It's how you learn where the danger was, and it's what keeps you training next week instead of icing an elbow.
- **Tell your partners you're new.** Good training partners adjust when they know. At Shonen, your partners want to see you succeed.
- **Choose consistency over intensity.** Two or three classes a week, every week, beats five classes one week and none the next. Progress in jiu jitsu comes from showing up.
- **Sit out a round when you need to.** If something feels tweaked, tell your coach and rest. There's no prize for grinding through it.
- **Take recovery seriously.** Sleep, water and a little mobility work on your off days will keep you on the mat longer than any supplement.
- **Mention old injuries up front.** Bad knee, cranky shoulder, past surgery? Tell the instructor before class so they can help you work around it, and check with your doctor if you're not sure you're cleared for exercise.

## What you get out of it

The adults who stick with jiu jitsu tend to come for one reason and stay for three.

### Real self-defense

Because jiu jitsu is trained against fully resisting partners, you find out what actually works. Royce Gracie showed the world at UFC 1 that a skilled grappler could beat a bigger striker, and BJJ has since become part of the defensive tactics curriculum of every major branch of the US Armed Forces.

### Fitness that doesn't feel like a chore

Flexibility, stamina, strength and balance all improve, but it rarely feels like a workout. You're too busy solving a problem to watch the clock.

### A team

Jiu jitsu is hard to do alone, so the people you train with become a big part of why you keep coming back. At Shonen, beginners and competitors train in the same room with the same respect, and our women's group is a big part of that.
